Manufacturing Downtime Analysis

I built a one-page Power BI dashboard for a soda bottling line: what keeps it from running at full speed, who and what is behind the downtime, and what to fix first. I used a public dataset, because my company's own data is confidential.

Role
Analysis & dashboard design
Tools
Power BI · Excel
Data
Public dataset from Maven Analytics, soda bottling line
Type
Portfolio project

01 · The question

What is the current line efficiency? Are any operators underperforming? What are the leading factors of downtime, and do some operators struggle with particular types of error?

02 · The approach

One page, and every chart title states its finding. Four KPI cards on top, a Pareto chart to find the few causes that matter, then a breakdown by operator and by product. Colour is used only for what needs action.

03 · The data model

A small, clean model: two fact tables (line productivity per batch, downtime per batch and factor) and two dimension tables (products, downtime factors), all related one-to-many. Every calculation sits in a separate measures table: line efficiency, total downtime, cumulative downtime and Pareto %, operator error ratio. A sort-order column keeps the Pareto bars in the right order. The dataset is small (38 batches), so the model is simple on purpose. The data covers only 5 days (29 Aug – 3 Sep 2024), so I left out a calendar table and trend charts.

05 · What I would do first

Start with the few causes that explain most of the loss.
  • Support Mac to raise overall efficiency. Mac has the lowest efficiency (61%) and the main root cause is batch change (130 min). I recommend training and mentoring, and a review of the process, so that Mac's workflow is not disproportionately harder than the others'.
  • Go after the leading downtime factors. Machine adjustment, machine failure and inventory shortage are the biggest causes. I recommend regular machine maintenance, better inventory management (especially for CO-600) and a training programme for the operators on machine adjustment.
  • Look into OR-600. Its efficiency (44%) is far below the other products (62–68%), so its production issues need a root-cause check.
